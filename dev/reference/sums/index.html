    Sums and recurrences#

    SymJ.harmonic — Tuple{Any}

    source 
    SymJ.harmonic(n)
SymJ.harmonic(n, m)

    The n-th harmonic number 1 + 1/2 + ... + 1/n, and the generalized one of order m, 1 + 1/2^m + ... + 1/n^m (harmonic(n, 1) is harmonic(n)): exact for an integer n, otherwise left as it is. Not exported.

    SymJ.summation — Tuple{Any, Sym, Any, Any}

    source 
    SymJ.summation(f, k, a, b; assume = nothing)

    The sum of f over the integers k = a, a+1, ..., b, in closed form where one is found. b may be Inf (an infinite series, whose value is found as a limit, so it may be Inf, or by cases of a parameter); a and b may be expressions. Assumptions about the other symbols are given with assume, as for limit.

    Polynomials in k are summed by Faulhaber's formula, hypergeometric terms (polynomials times r^k, factorials, binomials, rational functions that telescope) by Gosper's algorithm, and other rational functions through partial fractions, as harmonic numbers (generalized, SymJ.harmonic(n, m)) and polygamma functions (exact zeta values for even powers). Harmonic numbers times a term with an antidifference are summed by parts. A sum with no closed form is returned unevaluated, Sum(f, k, a, b). Not exported.

    SymJ.summation(k^2, k, 1, n)            # n^3/3 + n^2/2 + n/6
SymJ.summation(r^k, k, 0, n)            # (r^(n + 1) - 1)/(r - 1)
SymJ.summation(1/(k*(k + 1)), k, 1, Inf)  # 1
SymJ.summation(1/k^2, k, 1, Inf)        # π^2/6

    SymJ.rsolve — Tuple{Any, Sym, Vararg{Pair}}

    source 
    SymJ.rsolve(eq, y(n), inits...)

    The solution of a linear recurrence with polynomial coefficients in n, such as y(n+2) ~ y(n+1) + y(n), as a combination of hypergeometric terms (Petkovšek's algorithm Hyper). inits are initial values, y(0) => 0, y(1) => 1; without them the answer is in terms of y(0), …, y(d-1) for a recurrence of order d. The right side may also be a sum of hypergeometric terms in n (polynomials, 2^n, n!, (-1)^n). Not exported.

    When the hypergeometric solutions are too few, the order is reduced through one of them (d'Alembertian solutions): the answer is then in iterated sums of hypergeometric terms, such as harmonic(n) or an unevaluated Sum, and the right side may be any function of n. Failing that, the sections y(m N + i) (m up to the order) are solved each in those ways and interlaced (Liouvillian solutions), the answer piecewise in mod(n, m). When the solution is none of these (or Hyper's search is too large), the answer is the recurrence itself, DifferenceRoot(... ~ ..., y(0) => ..., ...), which gives its value at an integer by unrolling the recurrence (no arithmetic is done on it). An error when the equation is not a linear recurrence or the initial values do not fit its order.

    @syms n y()
SymJ.rsolve(y(n+2) ~ y(n+1) + y(n), y(n), y(0) => 0, y(1) => 1)   # Binet's formula
SymJ.rsolve(y(n+1) ~ 2y(n) + 1, y(n))                              # 2^n (y(0) + 1) - 1
SymJ.rsolve(y(n+1) - y(n) ~ 1/(n+1), y(n), y(0) => 0)              # harmonic(n)
SymJ.rsolve(y(n+2) ~ (n+1)*y(n), y(n), y(0) => 1, y(1) => 1)       # (n - 1)!!, by parity
