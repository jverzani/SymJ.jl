    SymJ.ParametricSolution

    A solution of a first-order equation in a parameter p, the slope y′ along the curve: x = X(p), y = Y(p). SymJ.dsolve gives one when p cannot be eliminated: y = 2x y′ + y′² is {x = C1/p^2 - 2p/3, y(x) = 2C1/p - p^2/3; p}. The fields are x and y (the expressions X and Y), p, and vars (x and y(x)). subs substitutes into both expressions; lambdify(s) is a function of p and then of the other free symbols (sorted), returning (X, Y).

    Not exported.

    SymJ.dsolve — Tuple{Relation, Sym, Vararg{Pair}}

    source 
    SymJ.dsolve(eq, y(x), ics...; assume = nothing)

    Solve the ordinary differential equation eq (an equation, written with ~) for the function y(x). Derivatives are written diff(y(x), x) or y'(x). The result is an equation: y(x) ~ S with constants C1, C2, … (one for each order), or an implicit solution φ(x, y(x)) ~ C1 when y cannot be solved for. An implicit solution quadratic in y gives a vector, one equation for each branch: y'(x) ~ (1 - x)/y(x) gives y(x) ~ ±sqrt(C1 + 2x - x^2). An equation that factors, with y in two or more factors (y'(x) (y''(x) + 1) ~ 0), gives the solutions of each factor, a vector of equations.

    A first-order equation nonlinear in y' gives a vector: the general solutions, then the singular solutions (from F = 0 and ∂F/∂y' = 0, those that solve the equation and are no member of the general solution; y'^2 + y^2 ~ 1 gives ±sin(x + C1) and ±1). Clairaut's y = x y' + f(y') gives the lines C1 x + f(C1) and the envelope; d'Alembert's y = x g(y') + f(y') and x = h(y') are solved in the parameter p = y', a SymJ.ParametricSolution {x = X(p), y(x) = Y(p); p} unless p can be eliminated simply; otherwise each branch y' = rᵢ(x, y) is solved. y' = f(ax + by + c) is separable in u = ax + by + c. With y(a) => b, the solutions through the point.

    Initial conditions are pairs, one for each order: y(0) => 1, y'(0) => 0. They fix the constants.

    With assumptions (assume = (k ≫ 0, m ≫ 0), an Assumptions, or inside assuming), a characteristic root they show to be complex gives cosines and sines: y'' = -(k/m) y is C1 cos(√(km) x/m) + C2 sin(√(km) x/m) with k, m > 0, and C1 exp(-√(-km) x/m) + C2 exp(√(-km) x/m) without.

    An autonomous equation y'' = f(y, y') whose y' has several branches (y' = ±sqrt(…), as for y'' = -sin(y)) gives a vector of solutions, one for each branch; with initial conditions, the branch through them (∫ᵥʸ dy/y' = x - a, a definite Integral(f, (y, v, y(x))), when the integral has no closed form).

    With method = :laplace, a linear equation with constant coefficients is solved by Laplace transforms (SymJ.laplace_transform); this is the method used, without asking, when the right side has SymJ.heaviside or SymJ.dirac. The initial conditions (constants for those not given) are at one point, and the solution holds from there on.

    With series = n, the solution is a series about x0 (the point of the initial conditions, else 0) with error O((x - x0)^n), returned as the truncated sum. At an ordinary point it is the Taylor series, for any equation that can be solved for its highest derivative, nonlinear ones included; a linear one is grouped by constant, C1*(…) + C2*(…). At a regular singular point of a second-order linear homogeneous equation it is the Frobenius series, with a log term when the indicial roots are equal or an integer apart.

    Methods: first order linear, separable, Bernoulli, exact (with an integrating factor in x or y alone), homogeneous, Riccati, Abel (y' = f₃y³ + f₂y² + f₁y + f₀, or (y + g) y' = f₂y² + f₁y + f₀ through y + g = 1/u, when the invariant Φ of the canonical form dz/dξ = z³ + Φ is constant: y' = y³ + y + e^(3x) is ∫ dz/(z³ + 1) = e^(2x)/2 + C1, z = e^(-x) y), Riccati or Abel in x(y) (y' = 1/(x² + y), in Airy functions of -y), and else a Lie point symmetry (from SymJ.infinitesimals; its integrating factor 1/(η - f ξ), or its canonical coordinates r, s, in which the equation is ds/dr = F(r): s - ∫ F dr = C1, a definite Integral from a point when ∫ F has no closed form; local, on one branch of r = R: y' = y²/(sin(y - x) - x² + 2xy) is -1/y - ∫₁^(y-x) dr/(r² - sin r) = C1), or a scaling symmetry (f(λx, λᵏy) = λ^(k-1) f, separable in u = y/xᵏ: y' = y⁴ + x^(-4/3) is ∫₀^(x^(1/3) y) du/(u⁴ + u/3 + 1) - log(x) = C1); linear with constant coefficients, of any order (undetermined coefficients, else variation of parameters, for the right side); Cauchy–Euler; Bessel's equation x²y'' + xy' + (α²x² - ν²)y = 0 (C1 besselj(ν, αx) + C2 bessely(ν, αx)) and the second-order homogeneous equations that reduce to it or to Airy's: y = e^(-∫p/2) v turns y'' + p y' + q y = 0 into v'' + I v = 0, and `I = A xᵐ

    and bessely, or besseli and besselk when A < 0; elementary for half-integer ν: x y'' + y' + y = 0 is C1 besselj(0, 2√x) + C2 bessely(0, 2√x)), I linear in x gives airyai and airybi (also Riccati equations made linear: y' = x + y²), and I = A + B/x + D/x² Whittaker's functions (in _₁F₁); hypergeometric equations, a₂ y'' + a₁ y' + a₀ y = 0 with a₀ constant, a₁ linear and a₂ linear (Kummer's, x y'' + (b - x) y' - a y = 0: C1 ₁F₁(a, b, x) + C2 x^(1-b) ₁F₁(a - b + 1, 2 - b, x)), constant (Hermite's) or quadratic with two roots (Gauss's, ₂F₁), after a linear change of variable: a polynomial solution when the series terminates (Legendre's, Laguerre's, Hermite's, Chebyshev's polynomials) and the second by reduction of order when that is elementary (Legendre's Q₆ in logarithms), else the two hypergeometric solutions; when they coincide (b or c an integer), only reduction of order (Legendre's equation for ν = 1/3 has no method: no Kummer U or Legendre Q); equations without y (reduced to a lower order); second order without x (autonomous: p = y' as a function of y); else a Lie point symmetry of a second-order equation y'' = ω(x, y, y') (ξ, η polynomials of degree at most 2 in x and y), whose canonical coordinates r, s reduce it to a first-order equation for v = ds/dr: x²y y'' = (y - xy')² is y = C2 x e^(C1/x), y'' + y'/x + eʸ = 0 gives two implicit solutions, one for each sign of v. Integrals are done by the RischIntegrate extension when it is loaded (an unevaluated Integral otherwise, or when there is no elementary antiderivative).

    Not exported.

    @syms x y()
SymJ.dsolve(y'(x) ~ y(x), y(x))                          # y(x) ~ C1*exp(x)
SymJ.dsolve(y''(x) + y(x) ~ 0, y(x), y(0) => 0, y'(0) => 1)   # y(x) ~ sin(x)
SymJ.dsolve(y'(x) ~ x * y(x)^2, y(x))                    # y(x) ~ -2/(x^2 + 2C1)
SymJ.dsolve(x^2 * y''(x) - 2y(x) ~ 0, y(x))              # y(x) ~ C1/x + C2*x^2
SymJ.dsolve(y''(x) - x * y(x) ~ 0, y(x); series = 6)      # C1*(1 + x^3/6) + C2*(x + x^4/12)
    SymJ.dsolve([eq₁, eq₂, …], [y₁(t), y₂(t), …], ics...; assume = nothing)

    A system of linear equations with constant coefficients, of any order. When the highest derivatives can't be solved for, a differential-algebraic system: an unknown may appear without derivatives (I_C + I_L = I), and a combination of the equations free of the highest derivatives, G = 0, is differentiated in place of one of them until they can be (Luenberger's shuffle), G = 0 fixing constants (A' = B - B' + c, A = B' + A' + d gives A = C1 e^(t/2) + d, B = C1 e^(t/2) - c); inconsistent or dependent equations are an error. The result is a vector of equations, with as many constants as the unknowns' orders add up to (fewer for a differential-algebraic system); the initial conditions fix them, one for each unknown and each order below its highest (at least as many as the constants for a differential-algebraic system, the others checked against the solution). A right side F(t) is integrated in closed form when it is made of polynomials, exponentials, sines and cosines. With assume, as for one equation, eigenvalues the facts show complex give cosines and sines (v' = -k u with k ≫ 0).

    @syms t u() v()
SymJ.dsolve([u'(t) ~ v(t), v'(t) ~ -u(t)], [u(t), v(t)])
# [u(t) ~ C1*sin(t) + C2*cos(t), v(t) ~ C1*cos(t) - C2*sin(t)]
SymJ.dsolve([u'(t) ~ v(t), v'(t) ~ -u(t)], [u(t), v(t)], u(0) => 1, v(0) => 0)
# [u(t) ~ cos(t), v(t) ~ -sin(t)]

    SymJ.infinitesimals — Tuple{Relation, Sym}

    source 
    SymJ.infinitesimals(eq, y(x); degree = 2, heuristic = :all)

    Infinitesimals (ξ, η) of Lie point symmetries of the first-order equation eq: the flow x ↦ x + ε ξ(x, y), y ↦ y + ε η(x, y) maps solutions to solutions, that is η_x + (η_y - ξ_x) f - ξ_y f² - ξ f_x - η f_y = 0 for y' = f(x, y). Found by SymPy's heuristics, each also applied to the equation for x(y), every candidate checked against the determining equation, the trivial ones (η = f ξ) left out:

    • :abaco1_simple: one of ξ, η zero, the other a function of x or of y alone, or a power times one (ξ = xᵏ B(y): an equation linear or Bernoulli in x(y));

    • :abaco1_product: one zero, the other F(x) G(y);

    • :bivariate: polynomials in x and y of total degree at most degree;

    • :chi: η - f ξ a polynomial of degree at most degree + 1;

    • :function_sum: one zero, the other F(x) + G(y);

    • :abaco2_similar, :abaco2_unique_unknown, :abaco2_unique_general: ξ a function of x, η of x or y, from patterns in f (f = A(x) + B(x) e^(y/C); powers of expressions in x and y; invariants of f).

    With heuristic, that one alone. A vector of pairs, in x and y(x); empty when none is found. SymJ.dsolve uses one when its other first-order methods fail: 1/(η - f ξ) is an integrating factor, else canonical coordinates r, s (ξ r_x + η r_y = 0, ξ s_x + η s_y = 1) make the equation ds/dr = F(r).

    Not exported.

    @syms x y()
SymJ.infinitesimals(y'(x) ~ (y(x)^3 + x^2*y(x) - x - y(x)) / (x*y(x)^2 + x^3 + y(x) - x), y(x))
# [(-y(x), x)]: rotations
