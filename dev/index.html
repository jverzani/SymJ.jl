    SymJ#

    Work in progress

    SymJ is under active development. Its interface may change, including in ways that break existing code, before a stable release.

    SymJ is a symbolic manipulation kernel written in pure Julia. It builds expressions in a canonical form as you go, keeps numbers exact, and is fast: construction, expansion, differentiation and substitution are within a small factor of SymEngine, and usually faster. Expressions are callable, so they can stand in for functions in numeric code, and they implement TermInterface, so generic term-rewriting code, such as GruntzLimit for limits and RischIntegrate for integrals, works on them.

    • The Tutorial works through the symbolic side of calculus with SymJ.

    • Implementation discusses the design choices: one node type, exact numbers, packed polynomials, and the gcd and factoring algorithms.

    • The Design notes record each decision in detail, with measurements.

    • The Reference lists the functions.

    General usage#

    using SymJ

    Symbolic variables and numbers#

    Variables are made with @syms, or with Sym from a name. Numbers stay exact: Sym makes an exact number, and arithmetic with symbols keeps integers and rationals exact.

    @syms x y z
p = (x + 1)^2 / 3
    \[\frac{\left( x + 1 \right)^{2}}{3}\] 
    sqrt(Sym(8)), sin(Sym(π) / 3), Sym(2)^100
    \[\left( 2 \cdot \sqrt{2}, \; \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \; 1267650600228229401496703205376 \right)\]

    A floating point number in an operation makes the result a float, so use 1//2, not 1/2, for an exact half:

    x / 2 == x * (1 // 2), x * (1 / 2)
    (true, 0.5*x)

    Expressions are built in canonical form#

    Sums collect like terms and products collect like bases as they are built, with no separate simplification step:

    x + 2x - y + x * y * x, exp(x) * exp(y), (x * y)^2
    \[\left( x^{2} \cdot y + 3 \cdot x - y, \; e^{x + y}, \; x^{2} \cdot y^{2} \right)\]

    Calling an expression#

    An expression is a Function. Called with a number, it evaluates with Julia's own arithmetic, so it works in numeric code (with dual numbers, BigFloat, intervals, …):

    u = x^2 - 2x
u(3), u(3.0), u(1 // 2)
    (3, 3.0, -3//4)

    Called with pairs, it substitutes; subs does the same:

    ex = x^2 * y + sin(x)
ex(x => 2), subs(ex, x => y + 1)
    \[\left( 4 \cdot y + \sin\left( 2 \right), \; y \cdot \left( y + 1 \right)^{2} + \sin\left( y + 1 \right) \right)\]

    Called with keywords, it gives the variables values by name. With all of them numeric the result is a Julia number, and otherwise an expression:

    ex(; x = 2, y = 3), ex(; x = 2)
    (12.909297426825681, 4*y + sin(2))

    A constant expression called with no arguments gives its value, and N gives a float:

    (Sym(2)^10)(), N(sqrt(Sym(2)))
    (1024, 1.4142135623730951)

    For speed in a loop, lambdify compiles an expression into a Julia function:

    f = lambdify(sin(x)^2 + 3x^5 - exp(-x / 2), x)
f(0.5)
    -0.45520193600547476

    Algebra#

    expand((x + y + 1)^3)
    \[3 \cdot x \cdot y^{2} + 3 \cdot x^{2} \cdot y + x^{3} + y^{3} + 6 \cdot x \cdot y + 3 \cdot x^{2} + 3 \cdot y^{2} + 3 \cdot x + 3 \cdot y + 1\] 
    factor(x^4 - 1), factor(x^2 * y^2 - 1)
    \[\left( \left( x + 1 \right) \cdot \left( x - 1 \right) \cdot \left( x^{2} + 1 \right), \; \left( x \cdot y + 1 \right) \cdot \left( x \cdot y - 1 \right) \right)\] 
    cancel((x^2 - 1) / (x + 1)), apart(1 / (x^2 - 1), x)
    \[\left( x - 1, \; \frac{-1}{2 \cdot \left( x + 1 \right)} + \frac{1}{2 \cdot \left( x - 1 \right)} \right)\]

    gcd works on polynomial expressions, and Poly gives the polynomial itself:

    gcd(x^2 - 1, x^2 + 2x + 1)
    \[x + 1\] 
    q = Poly(x^2 * y + 3x - 1, x, y)
degree(q), degree(q, y), nterms(q)
    (3, 1, 3)

    Equations and inequalities#

    An equation is written with ~; solve (from CommonSolve) finds its solutions. An inequality uses ≪ (typed \ll), ≦, ≫, ≧ or ≶, since < is kept for comparing numbers and returns a Bool.

    solve(x^2 - 5x + 6 ~ 0, x), solve(x^3 - 3x + 1 ~ 0, x)
    \[\left( \left[ 2, \; 3 \right], \; \left[ 2 \cdot \cos\left( \frac{10 \cdot \pi}{9} \right), \; 2 \cdot \cos\left( \frac{4 \cdot \pi}{9} \right), \; 2 \cdot \cos\left( \frac{2 \cdot \pi}{9} \right) \right] \right)\] 
    solve(x^2 ≫ 1, x)
    \[\left( -\infty, -1 \right) \cup \left( 1, \infty \right)\] 
    solve([x + y ~ 3, x - y ~ 1], [x, y])
    \[\left[ \begin{array}{l} \left\{ x = 2, \; y = 1 \right\} \end{array} \right]\]

    Calculus#

    diff differentiates; diff(f, x, n) gives the n-th derivative.

    diff(x^x, x), diff(sin(x)^2, x, 2)
    \[\left( \left( \log\left( x \right) + 1 \right) \cdot x^{x}, \; 2 \cdot \cos^{2}\left( x \right) - 2 \cdot \sin^{2}\left( x \right) \right)\]

    Limits are computed by GruntzLimit and integrals by RischIntegrate, two companion packages that work on SymJ expressions; limit comes with SymJ:

    Julia versions

    SymJ supports Julia 1.10 and later. RischIntegrate needs Julia 1.12 or later, so the integrals here need that version.

    using RischIntegrate
limit(sin(x) / x, x => 0), limit((1 + 1 / x)^x, x => Inf)
    \[\left( 1, \; e \right)\] 
    integrate(x * sin(x), x), integrate(1 / (1 + x^2), x)
    \[\left( - \cos\left( x \right) \cdot x + \sin\left( x \right), \; \arctan\left( x \right) \right)\]
