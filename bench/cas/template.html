Computer Algebra Comparison

SymJ, with GruntzLimit for limits and RischIntegrate for integrals, against SymPy and Giac on the everyday tasks of a computer algebra system: expansion, factoring, gcds, rational functions and partial fractions, derivatives, equations, limits and integrals. Every answer is checked numerically against the problem, in high precision, independently of the system that gave it.

What stands out

SymJ verifies 309 of the 311, Giac 305 and SymPy 296. Every limit and every integral is verified with SymJ (GruntzLimit, RischIntegrate). It misses two of the other problems:

  • (x² + 2√2 x + 2)/(x + √2): it treats √2 as an independent variable, so it does not use √2² = 2 to see the common factor. SymPy's cancel leaves it too; Giac's normal cancels it.
  • log x + log(x − 1) = log 6: it solves equations in which x appears through one function only, and here it is inside two logarithms.

Giac misses six.

  • Its expand((x + y)³(x − y)³) distributes the products but leaves terms such as x³(−y)³ uncombined. That is Giac's expand itself, the same from a parsed string, and normal gives the canonical form.
  • It raises an error on lim x→∞ xa/eˣ ("unable to make series expansion").
  • It returns ∫ t³/√(4 + t³), ∫ log t/(1 + t) and ∫ 1/√(1 + t³) unevaluated. RischIntegrate gives them with ₂F₁ and li2.
  • Its igamma answer to ∫ 1/log(t)1+n cannot be evaluated, so it is not checked.

SymPy misses fifteen.

  • One limit, lim x→a (xˣ − aᵃ)/(x − a): it raises an error, because it cannot decide the sign of aᵃ − exp(a log a).
  • Eleven integrals unevaluated, among them ∫ 1/(a sin x + b cos x)², ∫ √((a + x)/(a − x)), ∫ eᵗ log(1 + t) and the exponential integrals of Apostol 162–174.
  • One integral timed out: Apostol 55, ∫ √(1 + 3cos² x) sin 2x, over 60 s.
  • One answer marked wrong: its Piecewise answer to ∫ log t/(1 + t). On the branch used for t > 0 it is −iπ log(1/(t+1)) − polylog(2, t+1). With the principal value of polylog on its cut, which is how SymPy evaluates it, the two imaginary parts add instead of cancelling, so the derivative is not the integrand.

It also leaves the √2 fraction uncancelled, like SymJ.

Times, first calls. Giac takes 1.7 s in all and SymPy 126 s. SymJ takes 61.5 s, nearly all of it compilation: the first gcd (6.4 s), the first equation and the first linear system (4.0 and 3.5 s), the first limit (6.8 s) and the first integrals. Load times are 0.4 s for SymJ and 2.5 s for SymPy and Giac.

Times, repeated calls. SymJ takes 1.4 s in all, Giac 0.8 s and SymPy 126 s.

  • SymJ is fastest on expansion: 0.6 ms for the twelve, against 25 ms for Giac and 1.8 s for SymPy.
  • On factorization and gcds SymJ and Giac are about even (3 and 8–9 ms).
  • On limits SymJ is faster than Giac (556 against 669 ms; SymPy 4.7 s).
  • On integrals Giac is about 7x faster (122 ms against 849 ms; SymPy 119 s).

SymPy's repeat times equal its first: there is no compilation, and its cache is cleared before the repeat.

Where SymJ could gain. The largest cost is first-call compilation, most of it in gcds, equations, limits and integration. A PrecompileTools workload exercising these would cut much of it. Beyond that: cancellation over algebraic numbers (√2 as an algebraic number, not a variable), and equations through several functions of x (here by combining logarithms).

How this was run

The checks. An answer is verified when its value, computed by the system that gave it, matches the problem evaluated in plain Julia in 256-bit (derivatives: 4096-bit) floating point. Expansions, factorizations, rational functions and partial fractions are compared at two points with every variable given a value. A gcd must be a constant multiple of the true gcd. A derivative is compared with a high-precision difference quotient. Each solution of an equation must satisfy it, and a linear system's solution all its equations. A limit must match its known value, with parameters a = 1.7, b = 2.3. An integral's central difference at x = 0.31 and 0.57 must match the integrand to 1e-4, as in RischIntegrate's Apostol benchmark.

Correct, incomplete. The value is right but the form is not what was asked: an expansion with a different number of terms, a factorization into fewer irreducible factors, a reduced fraction whose denominator still has a common factor, partial fractions with too few terms, or fewer real solutions than there are. Not checked: the answer could not be evaluated numerically (it may still be right). Not done: the system returned the problem unevaluated (an Integral, a limit(…)) or no answer.

The problems. The limits are the 66 of GruntzLimit's limits benchmark (Gruntz's thesis, §8, and others) and the integrals the 175 of RischIntegrate's Apostol benchmark. The others were written for this comparison. For factoring and gcds the input is expanded first, untimed.

Times. Each system ran in its own process, one after another, with a limit of s per problem; after a timeout the process was restarted at the next problem. The first-call time includes compiling for the Julia packages (SymJ, GruntzLimit, RischIntegrate). The repeat time is a second call of the same problem, after clearing SymPy's cache; it was taken when the first call took under 15 s, and otherwise the first-call time is counted for it. Load time is the time to load the packages.
